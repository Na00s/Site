# Landscape motion

`scene.js` renders each source photograph into one WebGL canvas with local environmental motion. The central blue, `#739ad0`, is sampled from the original Claude daylight scene. Continuous brightness ramps retain photographic texture and depth. Warmth follows Fuji's photographed sunset and thin cloud streaks; forest fog, coastal light, hillside illumination, and tidal reflections each receive a softer scene-specific warm weight. Light blue, pale apricot-orange, warm cream, and a restrained pink tint keep the scenes close to the original sky. A small warm transition glow preserves airy atmospheric shading. Sampled reference colors and active stops are recorded in `reference-palette.json`.

Five gentle texture samples soften detail, and fine screen-pixel film grain varies at 24 frames per second. The original sky renderer remains independent. The editable `LANDSCAPE_SCENES` configuration contains each scene's crop, motion masks, and film controls: `exposure` adjusts luminance in stops, `softness` controls the sample radius in source pixels, and `grain` sets the peak-to-peak noise amplitude. Coordinates are normalized against the source photograph with `(0,0)` at its top left. `crop` selects the center used by a landscape viewport; `mobileCrop` selects the center for a narrow viewport. The renderer calculates an object-fit cover crop, with 1.8% extra room for restrained pointer movement. Reduced motion displays a static frame with the same color, softness, and grain treatment.

Each `water` or `water2` mask is a convex polygon with at most eight points, ordered clockwise as viewed in the photograph. The second polygon allows disconnected open-water regions. `waterFeather` softens a mask in photo coordinates. `waterTop` and `waterBottom` define perspective depth. `waterStrength` is displacement relative to photo width, and `waterBrightness` controls the small reflection variation. The displacement uses three independent wave directions and stays within the masked water. A luminance gate preserves dark branches and rocks; the aerial scene additionally favors blue-green water over warm sand.

`mistCenter` and `mistRadius` define a soft elliptical area over existing distant haze. Two slow, broad fields move within it at different rates. `mistStrength` limits their opacity, and `mistColor` is chosen from the source atmosphere. Forest mist also follows the photograph's existing bright fog regions so close trees remain clear. The hills add a moving broad shadow field at less than 3% contrast over the ground, with a light haze layer near the distant ridges.

The Fuji and coast configurations mask the visible water around the stationary shoreline and foreground. The forest uses a source-oriented crop to keep its misty treeline in a wide cover. The hills keep their distant coast and folds visible. The tides photograph has black sand at left and deep teal ocean at right; its animation stays in the open water beyond the surf edge. The lake photograph has a level treeline just past the middle; its water mask covers everything below it, and its mist drifts through the fog that already lies on the water, so the reflection below wobbles while the fog above it breathes. Its warmth follows the photographed sunrise, so the glow at the horizon and its reflection stay warm while the clouds and fog stay blue.

Animation runs at up to 30 frames per second with device pixel ratio capped at 1.5. It pauses when the cover leaves the viewport, the document is hidden, or reduced motion is enabled. Resize and reduced-motion changes redraw once. Pointer movement eases toward the cursor and stops affecting touch input. All listeners, observers, texture resources, and frames are released on disposal. Image loading callbacks ignore disposed instances, and context restoration rebuilds graphics resources.

The canvas receives the supplied `classes.canvas` class on creation and `classes.drawn` after the first complete draw, so the existing sky readiness observer continues to work. Load or graphics failures call `onUnsupported`. `setLook` preserves the shared daylight treatment and reports `day`; `setShelters` is intentionally empty because text positions should not reshape a photograph.
